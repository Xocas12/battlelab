## Reading these results

Hand-written interpretation. Every number below is a placeholder filled in from this run's tables (`battlelab report --notes-only` refreshes them), so the text cannot cite a number the tables no longer contain. These notes are statements about the model; most parameters are flagged assumptions.

**How far to trust each scenario.**

* **Hostomel.** It reproduces its history well: all four facts hold jointly in 60% of runs under Lanchester and 50% under the CRT.
* **Maleme.** It looks worse than in 0.2.0 (19% joint under Lanchester) because its first-landing anchor was tightened from "day 2" to "the afternoon of day 2", not because the model got worse. The commitment gate moved the first landings toward the afternoon, but most runs still land in the morning.
* **Ypenburg.** It reaches 62%. That is mostly because its control anchor was reread as "German troops on the field for at least 3 hours" rather than sole control, and partly because of the airborne ammunition limit and the second counterattack (#13). In 0.5.0 its wave sizes, wave times and Dutch counterattacks were sourced from the NIMH and WOH histories; that moved the joint share by about one point.

**What the factor swaps say.**

* **Hostomel with Maleme's factors.** Swapping all seven bundles raises P(airbridge) from 0.33 to 0.70. The largest contributions are Maleme's defenders (HOLD, -0.47) and German risk tolerance (RISK, +0.42), with the same signs under the CRT. RISK alone lifts Hostomel to 0.90. Maleme's defenders alone would have stopped the 2022 assault (0.02).
* **Maleme with Hostomel's factors.** The airbridge disappears (0.63 to 0.00). DENIAL is now the largest single contribution (-0.27); RISK is -0.18.
  * RISK used to be largest, and it shrank because of the bundling. The new commitment-gate parameters sit in the RISK bundle, so swapping in Hostomel's RISK also removes Maleme's commitment delay, which offsets part of the lower risk tolerance. This is the "the answer depends on the bundles" caveat of MODELING_STANDARDS §5, not a new finding about 1941. Swapped alone, Hostomel's RISK drops Maleme to 0.18.
* **Swaps involving Ypenburg.** Ypenburg's MASS bundle now carries the surprise shock and no-retreat morale, which is why Hostomel with Ypenburg's factors rises to 0.74.

**The Crete controls: what made Maleme different?** Heraklion and Rethymno share Maleme's Luftwaffe, HQ and airlift values, so a swap between them isolates what differed on the ground.

* **Maleme's garrison decides it, not its withdrawal.** The defenders' parameters are now two bundles: HOLD (garrison size, quality, entrenchment) and COMMAND (decision cycle, lost contact, fog, night moves).
  * Heraklion with all of Maleme's factors goes from 0.03 to 0.58, and Rethymno from 0.03 to 0.61.
  * In both, HOLD is the largest contribution (+0.40 and +0.36; under the CRT +0.21 and +0.18). COMMAND is small (+0.05 and +0.08).
* **The reverse holds.** Maleme with Heraklion's garrison alone falls from 0.63 to 0.01. With Heraklion's command behaviour alone (no command cycle) it stays at 0.59.
* **How big a garrison would have held Maleme** (section 5b):
  * P(airbridge) falls from 0.73 with 500 defenders at the field to 0.21 with 1,000, 0.04 with 1,500 and 0.01 with 2,000.
  * Without the command cycle the curve is almost the same at Maleme's own size (0.53 against 0.58 at 650). It is flatter for big garrisons (0.08 at 2,000), because turn-by-turn morale lets even a large garrison drift away, while a commander who judges the situation holds.
  * So in this model, the lost contact and night withdrawal explain *when* Maleme fell (the timing anchors), while the size of the force at the field explains *whether*.
* **Sanity check.** RISK contributes exactly +0.00, because the three share the same HQ values. Any other number would mean the swap machinery leaks.
* **Caveats.**
  * The finding rests on judged garrison sizes: 500-650 at Maleme, and 1,500-3,000 of Heraklion's 8,024 able to fight at the airfield. With a Heraklion garrison below about 1,000, the comparison would look different; see the sweep.
  * It is also a statement about this model's attrition and morale, not a historical verdict. The common reading stresses the 22nd Battalion's withdrawal, and the model does reproduce that withdrawal; it just does not need it to lose Maleme.
* **Weak anchors.** The Heraklion and Rethymno anchors hold in about 98-99% of runs, which only says the model lets strong, alert defenders win. Their garrisons and counterattacks are now taken from Long's official history (0.5.0), which did not move them.

**Valkenburg: the ground and the village, not the fight.** Valkenburg is the same operation as Ypenburg on an unfinished airfield that could not carry loaded Ju 52s.

* **The soft-ground mechanic works.** Many transports get stranded (99% of runs strand at least 20), and the Germans take the field (99%).
* **The recapture: the Germans left the field.** The Dutch retake the field in 44% of runs, and all four facts hold together in 33%.
  * In 0.4.0 the recapture came from an assumed ammunition limit (6-14 h) and a second counterattack. The sources (NIMH, WOH) contradict the first: the German pocket held out until 14 May, so the limit is now off.
  * With sourced values alone, the Germans hold the field in almost every run and the joint share is near zero. The sources explain why the field fell anyway: most of the Germans moved into Valkenburg village that afternoon, and the Dutch retook a lightly held field at 17:30.
  * The model now does that (`redeploy:`): 60-80% of the German troops on the field leave for the village 1-4 h after taking it. Both numbers are assumptions set before the anchor run, but the move itself is sourced. The second counterattack (H+11.5-12, sourced) then retakes the field.
* **The factor swap mostly reflects air losses.** Valkenburg with Ypenburg's factors moves from 0.99 to 0.76. Most of that is AIR (-0.20): the sources give Ypenburg heavier flak losses on the approach. HOLD adds -0.06. The metric here is P(airbridge), and Valkenburg's large first wave lands 500 troops in almost every run. What made Valkenburg's history different is not in the swap: the soft ground, the wide grass surface, the small second wave and the move into the village are all context (`ctx.*`), which swaps never exchange by design (MODELING_STANDARDS §5). Answering "why did Valkenburg differ from Ypenburg" needs a context comparison, not a factor swap.
* **Sources.** NIMH and WOH histories for the waves, garrison and counterattacks (0.5.0). One prior (`ctx.wreck_obstacle`) was revised after the first anchor run, and its note says so.

**Structural checks.** The Hostomel–Maleme swap conclusions do not depend on the go/no-go rule: see section 4, where threshold, logistic and lagged rules give Shapley values within a few hundredths of each other. The CRT remains illustrative (section 6); magnitudes under it should not be read.
