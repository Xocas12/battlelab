## Reading these results

Hand-written interpretation. Every number below is a placeholder filled in from this run's tables (`battlelab report --notes-only` refreshes them), so the text cannot cite a number the tables no longer contain. These notes are statements about the model; most parameters are flagged assumptions.

**How far to trust each scenario.**

* **Hostomel.** It reproduces its history well: all four facts hold jointly in 60% of runs under Lanchester and 50% under the CRT.
* **Maleme.** It looks worse than in 0.2.0 (20% joint under Lanchester) because its first-landing anchor was tightened from "day 2" to "the afternoon of day 2", not because the model got worse. The commitment gate moved the first landings toward the afternoon, but most runs still land in the morning.
* **Ypenburg.** It reaches 54%, but mostly because its control anchor was reread as "German troops on the field for at least 3 hours" rather than sole control. Its parameters are thinly sourced (issue #4).

**What the factor swaps say.**

* **Hostomel with Maleme's factors.** Swapping all six bundles raises P(airbridge) from 0.33 to 0.74. The largest contributions are Maleme's defenders (HOLD, -0.49) and German risk tolerance (RISK, +0.42), with the same signs under the CRT. RISK alone lifts Hostomel to 0.90. Maleme's defenders alone would have stopped the 2022 assault (0.00).
* **Maleme with Hostomel's factors.** The airbridge disappears (0.66 to 0.00). DENIAL is now the largest single contribution (-0.28); RISK is -0.18.
  * RISK used to be largest, and it shrank because of the bundling. The new commitment-gate parameters sit in the RISK bundle, so swapping in Hostomel's RISK also removes Maleme's commitment delay, which offsets part of the lower risk tolerance. This is the "the answer depends on the bundles" caveat of MODELING_STANDARDS §5, not a new finding about 1941. Swapped alone, Hostomel's RISK drops Maleme to 0.19.
* **Swaps involving Ypenburg.** These should be read as provisional until issue #4 is done. Ypenburg's MASS bundle now carries the surprise shock and no-retreat morale, which is why Hostomel with Ypenburg's factors rises to 0.58.

**Structural checks.** The Hostomel–Maleme swap conclusions do not depend on the go/no-go rule: see section 4, where threshold, logistic and lagged rules give Shapley values within a few hundredths of each other. The CRT remains illustrative (section 6); magnitudes under it should not be read.
