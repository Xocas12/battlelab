**How far to trust each scenario.**

* **Hostomel.** It reproduces its history well: all four facts hold jointly in {{anchor.hostomel_2022.lanchester.joint|pct}} of runs under Lanchester and {{anchor.hostomel_2022.crt.joint|pct}} under the CRT.
* **Maleme.** All four facts hold together in {{anchor.maleme_1941.lanchester.joint|pct}} of runs under Lanchester, and the first troop landings fall in the afternoon of day 2 in {{anchor.maleme_1941.lanchester.landings_begin_day2_afternoon|pct}}. That fit comes from the daylight confirmation (0.6.0): the New Zealanders left Point 107 at night, and the German HQ acted only after a test landing the next morning, then needed most of a day to embark and fly in a battalion. Under the CRT the joint share is still only {{anchor.maleme_1941.crt.joint|pct}}, because the CRT bleeds the Germans before the landings come.
* **Ypenburg.** It reaches {{anchor.ypenburg_1940.lanchester.joint|pct}}. That is mostly because its control anchor was reread as "German troops on the field for at least 3 hours" rather than sole control, and partly because of the airborne ammunition limit and the second counterattack (#13). In 0.5.0 its wave sizes, wave times and Dutch counterattacks were sourced from the NIMH and WOH histories. In 0.6.0 the ammunition limit was sourced too: WOH has the Germans short of ammunition by the late-morning truce, and the field defence collapsing before 15:10. That collapse time is now an anchor of its own ({{anchor.ypenburg_1940.lanchester.germans_off_field_by_1510|pct}} of runs), which is why the joint share is lower than in 0.5.0.

Swapped alone, Hostomel's RISK drops Maleme to {{single.maleme_1941__hostomel_2022.lanchester.RISK}}.

**The Crete controls: what made Maleme different?** Heraklion and Rethymno share Maleme's Luftwaffe, HQ and airlift values, so a swap between them isolates what differed on the ground.

* **Maleme's garrison decides it, through the 22nd Battalion's withdrawal.** The defenders' parameters are two bundles: HOLD (garrison size, quality, entrenchment) and COMMAND, the withdrawal's decision rule (decision cycle, lost contact, fog, night moves).
  * Heraklion with all of Maleme's factors goes from {{swap.heraklion_1941__maleme_1941.lanchester.base}} to {{swap.heraklion_1941__maleme_1941.lanchester.full}}, and Rethymno from {{swap.rethymno_1941__maleme_1941.lanchester.base}} to {{swap.rethymno_1941__maleme_1941.lanchester.full}}.
  * In both, HOLD is the largest contribution ({{shapley.heraklion_1941__maleme_1941.lanchester.HOLD|signed}} and {{shapley.rethymno_1941__maleme_1941.lanchester.HOLD|signed}}; under the CRT {{shapley.heraklion_1941__maleme_1941.crt.HOLD|signed}} and {{shapley.rethymno_1941__maleme_1941.crt.HOLD|signed}}). COMMAND is small ({{shapley.heraklion_1941__maleme_1941.lanchester.COMMAND|signed}} and {{shapley.rethymno_1941__maleme_1941.lanchester.COMMAND|signed}}).
  * A small COMMAND does not make the withdrawal minor. Swapping it changes how the battalion decides to leave, not whether it can: under Heraklion's rule it leaves on its own morale hazard instead of on a commander's order.
* **The reverse holds.** Maleme with Heraklion's garrison alone falls from {{swap.maleme_1941__heraklion_1941.lanchester.base}} to {{single.maleme_1941__heraklion_1941.lanchester.HOLD}}. With Heraklion's decision rule alone (no command cycle) it stays at {{single.maleme_1941__heraklion_1941.lanchester.COMMAND}}.
* **Without the withdrawal, Maleme holds** (section 5b, `maleme_garrison_no_withdrawal`). The Germans can take the field only after the 22nd Battalion has left it: control needs one side alone on the field, and Maleme's troop-carriers do not land on a contested one (`risk.land_contested` is 0). With voluntary departure switched off, no command decision falls inside the 48 hours and the battalion leaves only if it collapses. P(airbridge) is then {{linesweep.maleme_garrison_no_withdrawal.650}} at Maleme's own size (650), against {{linesweep.maleme_garrison.650}} as modelled, and {{linesweep.maleme_garrison_no_withdrawal.400}} with 400 defenders. Only a garrison of about 300 is destroyed often enough to lose the field without leaving it ({{linesweep.maleme_garrison_no_withdrawal.300}}).
* **How big a garrison would have held Maleme** (section 5b):
  * P(airbridge) falls from {{linesweep.maleme_garrison.500}} with 500 defenders at the field to {{linesweep.maleme_garrison.1000}} with 1,000, {{linesweep.maleme_garrison.1500}} with 1,500 and {{linesweep.maleme_garrison.2000}} with 2,000.
  * Without the command cycle the curve is almost the same at Maleme's own size ({{linesweep.maleme_garrison_no_command.650}} against {{linesweep.maleme_garrison.650}} at 650). It is flatter for big garrisons ({{linesweep.maleme_garrison_no_command.2000}} at 2,000), because turn-by-turn morale lets even a large garrison drift away, while a commander who judges the situation holds.
  * So in this model the garrison acts through the withdrawal, not instead of it. At Maleme's size the withdrawal is necessary: without it the field holds. A larger garrison takes fewer losses at better odds, so its commander is less likely to order it out before the paratroops are worn down. The decision rule (lost contact, night moves) mostly sets *when* the battalion leaves, which is what the timing anchors test.
* **Sanity check.** RISK contributes exactly {{shapley.heraklion_1941__maleme_1941.lanchester.RISK|signed}}, because the three share the same HQ values. Any other number would mean the swap machinery leaks.
* **Caveats.**
  * The finding rests on judged garrison sizes: 500-650 at Maleme, and 1,500-3,000 of Heraklion's 8,024 able to fight at the airfield. With a Heraklion garrison below about 1,000, the comparison would look different; see the sweep.
  * It is also a statement about this model's attrition and morale, not a historical verdict. The common reading stresses the 22nd Battalion's withdrawal, and the model agrees that Maleme was lost through it: at the battalion's sourced size the field falls only if the battalion leaves. What the model adds is that a much larger garrison would rarely have been ordered out.
* **Weak anchors.** The Heraklion and Rethymno anchors hold in about 98-99% of runs, which only says the model lets strong, alert defenders win. Their garrisons and counterattacks are now taken from Long's official history (0.5.0), which did not move them.

**Valkenburg: the ground and the village, not the fight.** Valkenburg is the same operation as Ypenburg on an unfinished airfield that could not carry loaded Ju 52s.

* **The soft-ground mechanic works.** Many transports get stranded ({{anchor.valkenburg_1940.lanchester.many_transports_stranded|pct}} of runs strand at least 20), and the Germans take the field ({{anchor.valkenburg_1940.lanchester.germans_take_field|pct}}).
* **The recapture: the Germans left the field.** The Dutch retake the field in {{anchor.valkenburg_1940.lanchester.dutch_retake_field|pct}} of runs, and all four facts hold together in {{anchor.valkenburg_1940.lanchester.joint|pct}}.
  * In 0.4.0 the recapture came from an assumed ammunition limit (6-14 h) and a second counterattack. The sources (NIMH, WOH) contradict the first: the German pocket held out until 14 May, so the limit is now off.
  * With sourced values alone, the Germans hold the field in almost every run and the joint share is near zero. The sources explain why the field fell anyway: most of the Germans moved into Valkenburg village that afternoon, and the Dutch retook a lightly held field at 17:30.
  * The model now does that (`redeploy:`): 60-80% of the German troops on the field leave for the village 1-4 h after taking it. Both numbers are assumptions set before the anchor run, but the move itself is sourced. The second counterattack (H+11.5-12, sourced) then retakes the field.
* **The factor swap mostly reflects air losses.** Valkenburg with Ypenburg's factors moves from {{swap.valkenburg_1940__ypenburg_1940.lanchester.base}} to {{swap.valkenburg_1940__ypenburg_1940.lanchester.full}}. Most of that is AIR ({{shapley.valkenburg_1940__ypenburg_1940.lanchester.AIR|signed}}): the sources give Ypenburg heavier flak losses on the approach. HOLD adds {{shapley.valkenburg_1940__ypenburg_1940.lanchester.HOLD|signed}}. The metric here is P(airbridge), and Valkenburg's large first wave lands 500 troops in almost every run. What made Valkenburg's history different is not in the swap: the soft ground, the wide grass surface, the small second wave and the move into the village are all context (`ctx.*`), which swaps never exchange by design (MODELING_STANDARDS §5). Answering "why did Valkenburg differ from Ypenburg" needs a context comparison, not a factor swap.
* **The context swap (section 3b) is lopsided, and the lopsidedness is the lesson.** Ypenburg with Valkenburg's factors and shared context goes from {{ctxswap.ypenburg_1940__valkenburg_1940.lanchester.base}} to {{ctxswap.ypenburg_1940__valkenburg_1940.lanchester.full}}, almost all of it CONTEXT ({{ctxshapley.ypenburg_1940__valkenburg_1940.lanchester.CONTEXT|signed}}): Ypenburg's waves are sized by `ctx.aircraft`, and Valkenburg's nominal 30 per wave (against 12) floods the field with troops. The reverse moves little (CONTEXT {{ctxshapley.valkenburg_1940__ypenburg_1940.lanchester.CONTEXT|signed}}), because Valkenburg sizes its waves through `ctx.wave1_n` and `ctx.wave2_n`, which Ypenburg does not have and which therefore cannot be swapped. A context swap only exchanges what both scenarios name the same way; read it as a lower bound, and check which parameters it listed as unswappable.
* **Sources.** NIMH and WOH histories for the waves, garrison and counterattacks (0.5.0). One prior (`ctx.wreck_obstacle`) was revised after the first anchor run, and its note says so.

**Waalhaven: the same morning, the opposite result.** Waalhaven is the Rotterdam half of the operation that failed at Ypenburg, flown by the same Luftwaffe command under the same plan. It reproduces its history in {{anchor.waalhaven_1940.lanchester.joint|pct}} of runs under Lanchester ({{anchor.waalhaven_1940.crt.joint|pct}} under the CRT).

* **The miss is speed.** The Germans take the field in almost every run ({{anchor.waalhaven_1940.lanchester.germans_take_field|pct}}), but within 2.5 hours in only {{anchor.waalhaven_1940.lanchester.field_taken_fast|pct}}; historically it was German by 06:30, about 1.6 hours after the drop. The model's paratroops win the field, just more slowly than III./FJR 1 did. No prior was adjusted after this run.
* **The factors explain little of the difference.** Ypenburg with all of Waalhaven's factors only moves from {{swap.ypenburg_1940__waalhaven_1940.lanchester.base}} to {{swap.ypenburg_1940__waalhaven_1940.lanchester.full}} (largest MASS, {{shapley.ypenburg_1940__waalhaven_1940.lanchester.MASS|signed}}: the better drop). Waalhaven with Ypenburg's factors still lands an airbridge in {{swap.waalhaven_1940__ypenburg_1940.lanchester.full}} of runs (from {{swap.waalhaven_1940__ypenburg_1940.lanchester.base}}; AIR {{shapley.waalhaven_1940__ypenburg_1940.lanchester.AIR|signed}}, MASS {{shapley.waalhaven_1940__ypenburg_1940.lanchester.MASS|signed}}). COMMAND contributes nothing since 0.7.0: neither defence has a command cycle (Waalhaven's was removed after its first anchor run, see the parameter note).
* **So in this model it was the airfield and the plan.** What the swap cannot exchange is context: Waalhaven was a large, firm civil field where wrecks were pushed aside (`ctx.wreck_obstacle` a fifth of Ypenburg's), and the transport stream kept coming all day (eight waves against three). At Ypenburg the first two waves' wrecks closed the strip. The caveat is that Waalhaven's wave count and spacing are assumptions; only the first wave's time is sourced.
* **The context swap confirms it, as far as it reaches** (section 3b). Adding the shared context as an eighth bundle, CONTEXT becomes the largest contribution in both directions: Ypenburg with Waalhaven's factors and context goes from {{ctxswap.ypenburg_1940__waalhaven_1940.lanchester.base}} to {{ctxswap.ypenburg_1940__waalhaven_1940.lanchester.full}} (CONTEXT {{ctxshapley.ypenburg_1940__waalhaven_1940.lanchester.CONTEXT|signed}}), and Waalhaven with Ypenburg's falls from {{ctxswap.waalhaven_1940__ypenburg_1940.lanchester.base}} to {{ctxswap.waalhaven_1940__ypenburg_1940.lanchester.full}} (CONTEXT {{ctxshapley.waalhaven_1940__ypenburg_1940.lanchester.CONTEXT|signed}}). The rest of the gap is structure no swap exchanges, mainly eight waves against three.
* **RISK contributes exactly {{shapley.waalhaven_1940__ypenburg_1940.lanchester.RISK|signed}}**, as it should: both scenarios carry the same German risk values.

**Ockenburg: the third Hague field, and the limits of P(airbridge).** Ockenburg was a makeshift field of football pitches held by 96 recruits. The Germans took it in a few hours and lost it the same afternoon. The model reproduces the sequence in {{anchor.ockenburg_1940.lanchester.joint|pct}} of runs under Lanchester ({{anchor.ockenburg_1940.crt.joint|pct}} under the CRT).

* **The recapture works, the clock does not.** The Dutch retake the field in {{anchor.ockenburg_1940.lanchester.dutch_retake_field|pct}} of runs, through the same mechanism as Valkenburg: most Germans leave the strip (`redeploy:`, here into the woods), and the second counterattack retakes what is left. But it is Dutch again by 14:30 in only {{anchor.ockenburg_1940.lanchester.germans_off_field_by_1430|pct}}; in most runs the last Germans hold on longer than the history allows.
* **One prior was revised after the first run**, and its note says so: the counterattacks' commitment time (`response.commit_h`) was 1-3 h, which made the decisive assault give up while winning.
* **The swaps say little here, by construction.** The swap metric is P(airbridge), at least 500 troops landed, and Ockenburg's strip can never take 500: its 28 aircraft carry 13-16 men each. Its base value is {{swap.ockenburg_1940__ypenburg_1940.lanchester.base}} and no factor from Ypenburg changes that. In the other direction Ypenburg moves from {{swap.ypenburg_1940__ockenburg_1940.lanchester.base}} to {{swap.ypenburg_1940__ockenburg_1940.lanchester.full}}, where HOLD ({{shapley.ypenburg_1940__ockenburg_1940.lanchester.HOLD|signed}}: Ockenburg's 100-120 recruits against Ypenburg's 250-450 defenders) is largely offset by MASS ({{shapley.ypenburg_1940__ockenburg_1940.lanchester.MASS|signed}}: Ockenburg's drop mostly went astray). The context swap with Valkenburg is all-or-nothing: Ockenburg with Valkenburg's context goes from {{ctxswap.ockenburg_1940__valkenburg_1940.lanchester.base}} to {{ctxswap.ockenburg_1940__valkenburg_1940.lanchester.full}}, because Valkenburg's first wave was 53 aircraft against Ockenburg's 18. For failure cases like these, a question about the recapture needs a different outcome metric; the swap machinery takes one (`swap --metric`), but the report runs P(airbridge) only.

**Structural checks.** The Hostomel–Maleme swap conclusions do not depend on the go/no-go rule: see section 4, where threshold, logistic and lagged rules give Shapley values within a few hundredths of each other. The CRT remains illustrative (section 6); magnitudes under it should not be read.
